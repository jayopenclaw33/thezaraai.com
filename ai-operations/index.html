The problem

The work is being done by hand, and nobody can prove what happened.

Not the strategic work. The daily mechanics that decide whether a lead is answered, a job is scheduled, or a report is right. Below is where the hours actually go in an operations team under $100M in revenue.

01 · Intake

Requests arrive in five places

Web form, phone, shared inbox, a chat channel, a message someone received personally. Whether a request gets answered depends on who happened to be looking at which app.

02 · Qualification

The rules live in one person’s head

Territory, urgency, budget, decision authority. Everyone applies them slightly differently, and nobody writes down why a request was passed over.

03 · Scheduling

Booking takes four messages

Offer times, wait, lose the slot, offer again. Meanwhile the calendar has capacity that nobody is looking at, and the customer has called a competitor.

04 · Reporting

Monday morning is spent rebuilding the numbers

Someone exports, pastes, reconciles, and formats. By the time the report is circulated it describes a week that is already over.

05 · Handoffs

Context is retyped at every boundary

Sales to operations, operations to delivery, delivery to invoicing. Each pass loses detail, and the loss only surfaces when a customer complains.

06 · The record

There is no record

When something goes wrong you can reconstruct roughly what happened by asking people. That is not a record. It is a recollection, and it arrives after the damage.

This is not a tooling problem, and adding an AI pilot on top of it does not fix it. 37% of organizations report using AI only at a surface level, with little or no change to underlying processes, and only 21% report a mature model for agent governance (Deloitte, State of AI 2026). Only 9% of organizations have fully deployed an AI use case (Accenture).

The risk is not using AI badly. It is running it without a record.

What gets built

Six deliverables. Each one names what you receive.

Agents run on the stack you already have — email and calendar, file storage, Slack or Teams, Notion, your CRM, your scheduling and ticketing tools. Nobody is asked to move into a new dashboard.

Deliverable 01

One intake queue

Every inbound route lands in a single queue with the source stamped on it. Missed calls trigger an immediate reply and continue by message.

You receiveA connected intake path per channel, with the routing rules written down and a named owner for each queue.

Deliverable 02

A qualification rulebook the agent applies

Territory, urgency, budget, and decision authority checked the same way every time. Anything failing a rule stops and goes to a person.

You receiveA written rulebook, the escalation paths, and the explicit list of decisions the system may never make alone.

Deliverable 03

Scheduling into real capacity

Only genuinely open slots are offered. Assignment follows your rules — route, skill, workload — and the confirmation and calendar hold are written immediately.

You receiveA live calendar integration, the assignment logic in writing, and the confirmation templates your team can edit.

Deliverable 04

Reporting that assembles itself

The numbers are written as the work happens rather than reconstructed on Monday. Definitions are fixed once so two people cannot produce two answers.

You receiveA scheduled report on the cadence you choose, plus a definitions sheet naming exactly how each figure is counted.

Deliverable 05

Handoffs that carry their context

The record moves between stages with everything attached. No retyping at a boundary, and no detail lost because the person who knew it was on leave.

You receiveA mapped handoff for each boundary, the fields that travel with it, and the exception path when a field is missing.

Deliverable 06

An audit trail on every action

What acted, on what basis, at what time, and what it touched. Written before go-live, not added after an incident makes someone ask for it.

You receiveAn audit configuration you could show an auditor, and an access inventory naming what each system can reach.

How it works

Five stages. A decision point at every one.

The same process the whole practice runs on. No stage begins before you have seen what the last one produced. You can stop at any gate and keep everything built so far.

01

Discover

A free 15-minute call, then a short working session. We walk your intake, your queues, and your Monday reporting, and rank the friction by what it costs you — usually not where people expect.

You receive
A written map of current state, friction ranked by cost.
Risk control
Read-only access. Nothing is changed, connected, or granted.
Your call
Do you agree with the diagnosis, and is the top item worth fixing?
02

Design

We specify the workflow before building it — triggers, qualification rules, exceptions, escalation paths, and the explicit boundary of what the system may never decide alone.

You receive
A workflow spec and data-flow diagram any competent engineer could build from.
Risk control
Data classes identified up front; sensitive material scoped out or protected.
Your call
Does the spec match how you would want a good employee to behave?
03

Build

Implementation against the spec, in test mode — your real tools, your real data shapes, but not your real customers until you say so.

You receive
A working system you can drive yourself, plus the test evidence behind it.
Risk control
Outbound is gated. Nothing reaches a customer while it is still being proven.
Your call
Having watched it run on your data, do you trust it to talk to a customer?
04

Secure

The stage most automation work skips. Access is narrowed to what each step needs, credentials move into managed storage, and the audit trail goes on before go-live. Deeper testing and governance sit in the Secure AI practice.

You receive
An access inventory and audit configuration you could show an auditor.
Risk control
The blast radius of any single compromised credential is established in writing.
Your call
Are you comfortable with what each system could reach if compromised tomorrow?
05

Operate

Go-live, then the unglamorous part: watching it, tuning rules as your season shifts, and keeping it working as vendor APIs move underneath it.

You receive
Ongoing operation, or a clean handoff to your team — your choice, stated up front.
Risk control
Patching and dependency review continue. An unmaintained automation is a liability.
Your call
Do you want us operating this, or do you want the keys?

Outcomes & evidence

What you hold at the end of it.

Automation is easy to demo and hard to trust. These are the artefacts an AI Operations engagement produces — the things that outlive the enthusiasm of week one.

Artefact

The running agents

The live workflows themselves, on your accounts and your credentials, doing intake, qualification, scheduling, reporting, and handoffs. You can switch any of them off without calling us.

Artefact

The audit trail

Every automated action attributable, timestamped, and reversible. When something goes sideways you can see what acted, on what basis, and when — before you have to explain it to anyone.

Artefact

The runbook

How to operate it day to day: what each workflow does, how to change a rule, what the alerts mean, what to do when a vendor API moves, and who to call in what order.

Artefact

The handover

Credentials, accounts, specs, and diagrams transferred to your team, written so they can operate and modify the system without us. Dependence is not a business model we are interested in.

Artefact

The workflow map

Every trigger, rule, handoff, exception, and escalation path documented — including what the system deliberately will not do on its own.

Artefact

The access inventory

A plain list of which system can reach which data, under whose credentials, with least-privilege applied and the remaining gaps named rather than hidden.

We do not publish customer counts, revenue impact, time-saved figures, or security outcomes on this site. Numbers like those are trivial to invent and impossible for you to verify, so treating their absence as a signal is reasonable. Client references are available on request and with client permission, matched to your sector. Ask on the call.
